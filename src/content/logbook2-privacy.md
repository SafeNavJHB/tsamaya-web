Logbook² is a pilot logbook app for iPhone and Android. This policy explains what happens to your information when you use it, and what your rights are under the Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 (POPIA). We have tried to keep it short, because the honest answer is simple: your logbook lives on your phone and we never receive it.

## Summary

- Everything you enter in Logbook² is stored on your device, in a local database. We never receive it.
- There are no accounts and no sign-in. We run no servers for the app.
- There is no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising and no tracking.
- We cannot see, recover or delete your data. Uninstalling the app deletes what it stored on your phone.
- Your logbook only leaves your phone in the ways listed in section 3, and in each one the destination is yours or the store's, not ours: files you share, automatic backups you turn on, your phone's own iCloud or Google backup, and flights you send to a student.

## 1. What stays on your device

Logbook² keeps the following in a local database on your phone:

- your flights, including dates, times, aircraft, routes, remarks and totals;
- your aircraft, and any places of your own, including their positions;
- your pilot name and licence number;
- your licences, ratings and medical expiry dates;
- photos and documents you attach, and signatures captured in the app;
- your duty log;
- if you instruct, your students' names, contact details, training records and the medical and licence expiry dates you enter;
- the app's settings.

It also keeps a few recent copies of your logbook on your phone, so a mistake can be undone, and keeps flights you delete for 30 days in Recently Deleted.

None of this is sent to us, and we have no way to read it. Because we never receive it, we do not collect, store or process it as a responsible party under POPIA. If you keep your students' details in the app, you are responsible for that information.

If you add a home-screen widget, it shows your total hours, your recency and your next expiry on your home screen. Turning on app lock removes them from the widget.

## 2. What we do not do

- We do not create accounts for you, and you do not sign in.
- We do not run servers that receive your logbook or anything else from the app.
- We do not use analytics, crash reporting or advertising, and we do not track you across apps or websites.
- We do not sell or share personal information, because we do not have any.
- We do not use your contacts or microphone.

The app uses three things on your phone only when you ask it to:

- **Location.** When you tap Use my location to save a farm strip or helipad. The position is saved with your logbook, so it is also in your backups. It is not tracked, and it is never sent to us.
- **Camera and photos.** When you choose to scan a logbook page or attach a photo.
- **Face ID, fingerprint or passcode.** If you turn on app lock. Your phone checks them, and the app only learns whether it succeeded.

## 3. What can leave your device

- **Subscription purchases.** Logbook² Pro is bought through the App Store (Apple) or Google Play (Google). Apple or Google takes the payment under its own terms and privacy policy. The app receives from the store only whether a subscription is active and when it expires. We never see your card or other payment details, and we do not receive your name or email address from the store.
- **Files you share or print.** You can export your logbook as JSON, Excel or PDF, or print it. These go through your phone's share sheet, and the file goes wherever you send it: Files, iCloud Drive, Google Drive, email, a printer or another app. Once a file leaves Logbook², it is governed by the service you sent it to, not by us.
- **Automatic backup on iPhone, if you turn it on.** Logbook² saves your flights and records to a folder you choose in the Files app, usually in iCloud Drive. These files leave out photos and have no password. They are governed by whichever service holds that folder.
- **Your phone's own backup.** If Backup is on in your phone's settings, Android keeps your flights and records in your Google account, without photos, and an iPhone's iCloud backup includes the app's data. This is between you and Apple or Google, under their terms. We cannot read these backups.
- **Flights you send to a student.** If you instruct, you can send a flight as a QR code or a link. It holds the flight's date, registration, aircraft type, route, times, hours, landings and lesson details, and your name. It does not hold your remarks or your student's details. The link goes to a small page on our website, which hands the flight to the app. The flight's details are in the part of the link after the # sign, which browsers do not send to a website, so we never receive them. Our website's host receives the ordinary details of any page visit, such as an IP address. Anyone you send the link to can read what is in it.
- **Feedback you send.** Send feedback opens your own email app with the app version and your phone's system version filled in. You see it before you send it.
- **Google ML Kit statistics, on Android.** Reading a photo of a logbook page uses Google's ML Kit on your phone. The photo and its text stay on your phone, but ML Kit sends Google usage and performance statistics, such as the device model and how long recognition took.
- **Network details sent to the stores.** When the app contacts the App Store or Google Play, for example to check a subscription, Apple or Google receives the standard network details any app sends, such as your IP address and basic device information. This is handled under Apple's or Google's privacy policy.

Apple and Google may process information outside South Africa. That processing is theirs, under their terms.

## 4. Importing

Importing reads a file that you pick yourself: a PDF exported from another logbook app, an Excel file, a CSV file or a Logbook² backup. The file is read on your phone and the flights from it are added to the local database. Nothing is uploaded.

**Scanning a paper logbook.** You can photograph a page of your paper logbook, or choose a photo you already have. The app asks for camera or photo access only when you choose this. Text recognition runs on your phone, using the recognition built into iOS (Apple Vision) or Google ML Kit's on-device model on Android (section 3); the photo is not sent to us or to anyone else. The photo is kept only in the app's temporary storage while you check the result, and the flights you confirm are saved to the local database like any other entry. Logbook² does not keep the photo afterwards, though a photo you took with the camera may also be in your phone's own photo library under your phone's rules.

## 5. Backups

We hold no copy of your logbook, and we cannot restore your data if you lose both your phone and your backups. Please keep a backup somewhere safe.

A backup file is one you create from the app and keep wherever you choose. You can protect it with a password, in which case it is encrypted on your phone. We do not have the password: if you forget it, nobody can open that backup. Automatic backup and your phone's own backup are described in section 3.

## 6. Keeping and deleting your information

Because your information is only on your device, you control how long it is kept.

- Deleting a flight moves it to Recently Deleted, where it stays for 30 days and is then removed. Other entries are removed when you delete them. Copies kept for undoing mistakes are replaced as newer ones are made.
- Uninstalling the app deletes everything it stored on your device.
- Files you exported and automatic backups stay where they were saved until you delete them there.
- If you use your phone's own system backup, that copy is governed by your device maker's settings and terms. Deleting the app from your phone does not necessarily remove a copy held in such a backup.

## 7. Security

Your logbook is protected by the security of your phone, such as its passcode or biometric lock and the operating system's protections. You can also turn on app lock, which asks for your phone's own lock when you open Logbook² and hides its contents in the app switcher. Because we hold nothing, there is nothing of yours on our systems to be breached. Please protect your phone, and the backup files you create, as you would any other record of your licence and flying history.

## 8. Your rights (POPIA)

Under POPIA you have the right to:

- ask whether we hold personal information about you, and for a copy of it;
- ask us to correct or delete it;
- object to our processing it, and withdraw any consent you gave;
- lodge a complaint with the Information Regulator (section 9).

Since we hold none of your logbook information, you exercise these rights directly on your device. You can see all of your data in the app, correct any entry by editing it, and delete any entry in the app. Uninstalling the app deletes everything. If you would like to confirm this, or you have a question about your rights, write to us at info@tsamayaapp.co.za and our Information Officer will reply.

## 9. Complaints

We would like the chance to put things right first, so please contact us at info@tsamayaapp.co.za. You also have the right to complain to the Information Regulator of South Africa at inforeg@justice.gov.za.

## 10. Children

Logbook² is not directed at anyone under 18, and we do not knowingly process children's personal information. We do not collect personal information from anyone through the app.

## 11. Your logbook is your own record

Logbook² is not approved, endorsed or certified by the South African Civil Aviation Authority (SACAA), and you remain responsible for your logbook and for keeping the records the regulations require. Our Terms of Use explain this in full. For privacy purposes the point is that your records are yours: we do not hold a copy, so we cannot supply one to you, a regulator or anyone else.
