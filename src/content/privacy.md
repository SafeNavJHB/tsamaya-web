# Tsamaya Privacy Policy

**Version 1.15. Effective date: 11 October 2026.**

**Responsible party (POPIA):** Tsamaya (Pty) Ltd, a private company registered in South Africa, registration number 2023/990736/07 ("we", "us")

**Address:** 10 Audocia Place, Hurlingham Manor, Sandton, 2196, South Africa

**Information Officer:** Kyle Kimble (director)

**Contact for anything in this policy:** info@tsamayaapp.co.za

Tsamaya is a navigation app for South African metros that suggests driving routes with lower statistical exposure to areas and roads carrying elevated, statistically derived risk. This policy explains what personal information the Tsamaya app and our website (tsamayaapp.co.za) process, why, who receives it, how long we keep it, and your rights under the Protection of Personal Information Act, 2013 (POPIA). Earlier versions are listed at the end.

## The short version

- Your location is used **on your phone** to show the map, guide you and plan routes. To do that, coordinates go to the services that answer: Mapbox for maps and routes, Google, HERE or Mapbox for place search, and our own server to find police and roadblock notices near you and for the rain reminder. They go **without your name or an account**, and we keep **no history of where you are or where you go**, except what you choose to send us (sections 3 and 3a).
- We run **no user accounts** for drivers, **no advertising**, **no sale of personal information**, and **no tracking across other apps**.
- Saved places (like Home and Work), recent searches, saved routes and your trip history are stored **only on your phone**. We cannot see them. A place name leaves your phone only in the ways this policy describes, such as a search, the natural voice saying where you have arrived (section 3c), a live trip you choose to share (section 3a) or a report you send (section 3).
- Your installation of Tsamaya has a **random installation identifier**. It is not your name or an account, but it links the usage events, crash reports, reports, notification registration and shared trips that come from the same installation, so we treat it as personal information.
- We collect **usage events** (such as a route being accepted) and **crash reports**, linked to that identifier, never your name or where you went. You can switch usage events off in Settings › Sharing & privacy. See section 4.
- If you choose to **send a report or feedback**, we store what you submit (and your email only if you choose to provide it). See section 3.
- **Voice reports are optional.** When you tap **Speak** (or ask Siri) to report something on the road, your phone's own speech service turns what you say into text so the app can choose the report. Tsamaya **never records or keeps audio**; only the report itself is sent. See §3b.
- **Tsamaya Premium is optional.** If you subscribe, Apple or Google takes the payment and **we never see your card**. RevenueCat, the service that checks subscriptions for us, receives your store purchase records and a random id it creates, so Premium stays on the account that paid. See section 4b.
- **Notifications are optional**: nothing arrives unless your phone lets Tsamaya send them, and news about new features only if you ask for it. We store a delivery token for your device and the metro you are in (such as "Cape Town"), **never your coordinates**. With the fuel price alert on, we also store your fuel type, and with Tsamaya Premium roughly until when you have it. Each kind has its own switch. See section 4a.

## 2. What we deliberately do not do

- No server-side history of your location or trips. What our server holds is listed in sections 3, 3a, 4 and 4b: reports you choose to send (some of which carry a position), a live trip you choose to share (deleted within 30 days after its link stops working), and positions used for a single lookup and then discarded.
- No advertising or ad-tech SDKs, and no advertising identifier.
- No sale or sharing of personal information for anyone's marketing.
- No decision about you with legal or similarly significant effects based solely on automated processing (POPIA s71), and no profiling for advertising. One automated check affects notifications only: an installation that behaves like an app-store review or test device stops receiving notifications. If you think this has happened to you, contact us and we will switch them back on.
- No audio recording. The microphone is used only while you are making a voice report (§3b), and nothing it hears is kept.

## 3. User reports and feedback

Tsamaya lets you suggest updates to risk areas and roads, rate trips, report what you see on the road, and send feedback or bug reports, all **without an account**. When you submit a report we store:

- your selections: the risk tier you suggest, the kinds of incident you select, and the timing you choose;
- whatever you type in the note or message field (**please do not include personal information about yourself or others**); a report made during a drive fills in the road you were driving on, and one made from the car screen or through Siri also your speed and direction of travel at that moment;
- the area or road the report concerns (and any line or area you draw, with the points you tapped), the time band, and the app version;
- a **random installation identifier** generated on your device, used to spot duplicate or abusive submissions and to find your reports if you ask us about them; it is not an account and identifies the installation, not you;
- your **email address only if you choose to provide it**, used only to reply to that report.

**Trip ratings** store coarse trip statistics (such as a distance bucket, the time band, the route option and whether a reroute happened) and, for each rated area or road the drive passed through, its name and your answer, **never your start or end locations**. A rating is sent when you tap Good or Not good (with any details you then add), or when you close the rating after answering at least one question.

**"This route looks wrong"** reports store your comment, the destination's name, the route option, its distance and time, the metro, the time band, the app version and the random installation identifier, together with the start and end rounded to about 100 m, the lines of the routes we offered without their first and last 200 m, and the names of the risk areas and roads each route avoided or passed. The review team uses them to find and fix routing mistakes.

**A navigation log you send** from Settings › Help & About (Help & feedback) helps us find a fault. You choose the version without locations, or the full log, which includes positions and place names from your recent drives. It goes by email to admin@tsamayaapp.co.za with your installation identifier and a summary of your settings.

Reports are suggestions for human review; they never change the live dataset automatically, except for speed cameras: a camera reported at the same spot from three different installations is added to the map without waiting for review, and drivers' answers to "still there?" on a reported camera extend, shorten or end how long it shows. Each such answer stores the random installation identifier, so one installation counts once. When a report arrives, the review team is told by email (sent through Resend) and by a notification on their phones (sent through Expo), which can carry the report's text and place name.

Some reports are also checked by Claude, an AI assistant made by Anthropic, to help the review team, through Anthropic's API under its commercial terms, which do not allow Anthropic to train its models on what it receives: area and road reports and trip ratings with details automatically, and other reports, such as crime spot reports, when the team asks. Claude receives what the report says, its area, road or pinned point and the street address nearest to it, its timing, and what the app already holds there, such as the current rating or nearby hotspots. It never receives your installation identifier or email address. Claude may search public sources, and its answer is advice for the review team: it never approves or declines a report by itself. Anthropic's own terms and Privacy Policy (www.anthropic.com/legal/privacy) apply to what it receives.

Some kinds of report carry a position, because the position is the report:

- **Police and roadblock notices** are public: other drivers nearby see "Police reported ahead" for about an hour. We store the spot where your car was when you reported, its direction of travel and the time. Your random installation identifier and a scrambled form of your network address (a hash under a secret key that only our server holds, so nobody without that key can turn it back into the address) are kept apart from the notice, only to merge repeat reports and to limit abuse, and are never shown to anyone. Notices are deleted about a day after they expire. If you pass a police or roadblock notice another driver reported, Tsamaya may ask you "still there?". Your answer is stored with your random installation identifier and the same kind of scrambled network address, only to count each installation once and to limit abuse; neither is ever shown to anyone, and both are deleted with the notice. Answering "Still there" keeps the notice up for another hour, up to four hours after it was first reported. A **roadblock** report is also filed for the review team as a report under this section (the road you were on, from the car screen or Siri your speed and direction, the time band and your installation identifier), kept as other reports are (section 6).
- **Speed camera reports** store the spot you reported, the kind of camera, the metro, the speed limit if you gave one, any comment you add, and the random installation identifier, for the review team, which confirms or rejects the camera (section 6 says how long the report is kept).
- **Crime spot reports** (a hijacking or smash-and-grab spot you pin on the map) and suggested corrections to a hotspot's position store the spot you pinned, not where you are, with the place name nearest to it, the kind of spot, when it happened if you say, your comment and the random installation identifier, for the review team. An approved spot is shown on every driver's map with a name and safety tip chosen by the review team; your comment and identifier are never shown.

## 3a. Live trip sharing, SOS and Guardian (optional)

Tsamaya lets you **optionally** let someone you choose follow your drive on a private web link. Nothing is shared unless you start it:

- **Share** during a drive creates a link for that trip and opens your phone's share sheet, so you choose who receives it.
- **SOS › Alert** your emergency contact creates an emergency link and opens your phone's messaging app with a message to them, which you send. The message contains your exact position as a Google Maps link and the emergency link.
- **Guardian** uses one lasting link for your installation, which you send once to someone you trust. After that, each time you switch Guardian on (for example from the car screen), your drive is published to that same link until you switch it off or arrive.

While a trip is shared we store, on our server (Supabase): your **current location and heading**, your **destination (its position and name) and estimated arrival time**, the **route line you are driving**, which starts where you set off and so shows your starting point, and the **random installation identifier**, but never your name or an account. If someone opens the link inside the Tsamaya app, their own installation identifier is stored with the trip so that their app can follow it. The trip is readable **only by someone who holds the link** (a random, unguessable token), so share it only with people you trust.

When you end the trip or arrive, live updates stop and the private link **expires about 30 minutes later**. While a trip is still active, the link stays viewable for **up to 12 hours after your last update** (emergency **SOS links last up to 24 hours**, so a helper can keep checking on you). We delete the trip's stored location, destination and route within 30 days after its link stops working, and sooner on request via the contact address. Apart from police and roadblock reports and the reports in section 3 that carry a position, this is the only case where your location is stored on our server.

## 3b. Voice reports and Siri (optional)

If you tap **Speak** in the report screen (on your phone, CarPlay or Android Auto), Tsamaya asks for microphone and speech recognition access, then listens for a few seconds for what you say, such as "police" or "speed camera". Your phone's own speech service turns the words into text: Apple's speech recognition on an iPhone, and the speech recognition service on an Android phone (usually Google's). Depending on the phone and the language, that service may process the audio on the phone or on the provider's servers, under the provider's own privacy terms.

Tsamaya uses the text only to choose the report type, and then files exactly the report a tap would (see section 3). **We do not record, store or send the audio, and we do not keep the text.** If you ask Siri instead ("report police in Tsamaya"), Siri does the listening and passes Tsamaya only the report type. If you ask Siri for directions in Tsamaya, Siri (Apple) hears the place you name and passes it to Tsamaya, which looks it up like any search (section 1). On Android Auto, a destination you say is heard by Google Assistant, which passes Tsamaya the words in the same way.

You can refuse or withdraw microphone and speech access at any time in your phone's settings. Every report can still be made by tapping.

## 3c. Spoken directions (natural voice)

With **Natural voice** on (Settings › Navigation, on unless you switch it off), each sentence Tsamaya speaks is made by Google Cloud Text-to-Speech through our server. Most sentences are directions such as "Turn left onto Jan Smuts Avenue". The closing sentences say where you have arrived, but in this voice the only destination or stop names they use are Home and Work: any other destination or stop (a street address, a place you named) is "your destination" or "your stop", so it never leaves your phone this way. Your phone's own voice, which sends nothing, still says the name.

Our server keeps the audio of each sentence in a cache it shares between drivers, so that the same sentence is never made twice. The cache stores a sentence's sound under a code made from its words, with no installation identifier and nothing else about you or your device, and it cannot be listed or browsed. Google receives only the sentence, from our server, never anything about you. Versions of the app before its October 2026 update could also send the name of a destination or stop in the closing sentence; our server now refuses such a sentence (the phone then says it in its own voice), and the clips those versions made stay in the cache, which cannot be listed and links them to no one. Switch Natural voice off and Tsamaya uses your phone's own voice, which sends nothing.

## 4. Usage analytics and crash reporting

Tsamaya records **usage events** to help us understand whether people find it useful, what to improve and what to fix. Examples include the app being opened, a route being requested, shown, accepted, started, completed or abandoned, a reroute, a report being sent, a route being shared, and the Premium screen being shown or a purchase being made. These events carry only **short, coarse details** (such as the metro you are in, the route option chosen, rounded distances and durations, the safety grades of the options, and whether a route was rerouted), together with the random installation identifier described in section 3, a random number for that app session, the app version, the build channel and the platform. They **never** include your name, an account, your start or end locations, place names, or precise coordinates. We also keep a record of each installation (when it was first seen, its platform, app version and metro) so that we can count installations. We use these events to understand use in aggregate, to tell the team when a new installation first opens the app (with its platform and metro), and to recognise app-store review and test devices (section 2); never to profile you for advertising or to decide anything else about you. **You can switch usage events off at any time** in Settings › Sharing & privacy (Share usage data).

**Crash reporting is enabled.** When the app crashes or hits an unexpected error, it sends a technical report to Sentry so that we can find and fix it. Without it, a crash you hit is simply a crash we never learn about.

A crash report contains the error and its stack trace, your device model and operating system version, the app and update version, the random installation identifier described in section 3, and whether each app session ended normally. It also carries a short trail of recent app events (a reroute, a change in risk level, connecting to a car screen) and of the services the app recently called, so that we can see what led up to the failure.

That trail is **filtered on your phone before anything is sent**: place names, your starting point and destination, searches and coordinates are stripped out, a web request keeps only the service's name (such as "api.mapbox.com/directions"), never the rest of its address, and only simple values are allowed through. Versions of the app before its October 2026 update sent the full address of recent web requests, which for map, route and search requests can contain coordinates or the text of a search. A crash report never contains your name or an account. Sentry stores this data in the **European Union**.

We use crash reports only to fix faults. They are not used to profile you and are not shared for any other purpose.

## 4a. Push notifications (optional)

If you allow notifications, Tsamaya can tell you about road closures, protests and race-day disruption in your city. A second, separate switch covers news about new app features, and a third tells you, before fuel prices change, whether to fill up or wait: with Tsamaya Premium usually on the Monday before the change, otherwise at noon the day before. Nothing arrives unless your phone lets Tsamaya send notifications (you are asked first; older Android versions allow it when the app is installed). Once it does, the road closure switch is on until you switch it off. **News about new features is sent only if you ask for it** ("Turn on, with app news" when Tsamaya first asks, or its switch in Settings); our server sends app news only to a device whose driver chose it, so a version of the app from before October 2026, which cannot record that choice, gets none. Each switch can be turned off on its own at any time under Settings › Alerts › Notifications (the fuel price alert is on until you turn it off, except that on a device without Premium where you have not set it yourself, it follows the other two switches: on while either is on, off when both are; on a device set up before the October 2026 update, it stays on as though you had chosen it, as it was then). Turn all of them off, or block Tsamaya's notifications in your phone's own settings, and we delete this device's registration (in the second case, the next time you open the app).

To deliver a notification we store, for each device:

- the push token that Expo, Apple or Google issues for that installation (a delivery address for the device, not a name, an account or a contact detail);
- the random installation identifier described in section 3, so that a device which re-registers is recognised instead of duplicated;
- the platform (iOS or Android) and the app version;
- the metro you are in: a city name such as "Cape Town", chosen from the metros we cover, so that a Cape Town closure alert does not go to drivers in Johannesburg. **Not coordinates, not a street, not a trip.**
- which of the switches are on (road closures, app news and the fuel price alert), and when you chose app news;
- with the fuel price alert on: the fuel you chose, such as petrol 95, so that the alert names your fuel (both fuels when Premium's logbook has two vehicles on different fuels; none until you choose one, and the alert then names petrol 95 and diesel 50 ppm), and with Premium a date until which this device should get it first: about a month after the current Premium period ends when the plan renews (so a renewal never interrupts the alert), the day after it ends when it will not renew, and about a month ahead for Premium with no end date. The app sends that date again each time it starts. With the alert off, neither is stored.

Every notification comes from Tsamaya, and each one can be stopped with its switch in Settings. We do not use notifications for anyone else's advertising, and we do not send them for anyone else. A person writes and approves every notification before it goes out, except the fuel price alert, which you can switch off: it is drafted automatically from the official announcement of the new prices and normally approved by a person; it goes out once per price change, first to Premium devices and at noon the day before the change to every other device with it on. Nothing is sent because of where you are or where you drive. If the delivery service tells us a token no longer works, for example after you uninstall the app, we stop using it.

## 4b. Tsamaya Premium (optional subscription)

Tsamaya Premium is an optional subscription (Terms of Use section 6). If you never subscribe, the only part of this section that applies to you is that the app asks RevenueCat whether this device has Premium, which sends RevenueCat the random id and device details described below. On an iPhone, the first such check after installing also sends Apple's record of the app's own download (not a payment), so that a subscription already on the same Apple ID comes back without a tap.

When you subscribe, you pay Apple (App Store) or Google (Google Play) under their own terms. **We never see your card or bank details, your name or your email address.**

To check that a subscription is real, and to keep Premium on the right account, the app uses **RevenueCat**, a subscription service. RevenueCat receives:

- a random **RevenueCat id** that the app creates on your device. It is not an account, and it is not the installation identifier in section 3;
- the store's **purchase and renewal records** for Tsamaya Premium: which plan, when it started, when it renews or ends, and whether a free trial or a billing problem applies;
- basic **device details** sent with each request: the device model, operating system and app version, language and store country. On an iPhone, RevenueCat's software also sends Apple's **identifier for vendor**, an id that is the same for every app from one developer on that phone. Tsamaya does not give RevenueCat any advertising identifier.

On Android, Google's backup service may keep a paying subscriber's RevenueCat id so that Premium comes back after a reinstall.

RevenueCat tells our server (Supabase) when a subscription starts, renews, ends or moves to another phone. We keep that status (the RevenueCat id, the plan, the store, the dates and RevenueCat's message about each change) so that we can count subscribers and answer billing questions. It contains no name, email or location.

Premium's more detailed place searches go through our server to Google with your RevenueCat id, so that our server can count how many Premium searches that id made each day (the count is deleted after 35 days); the search itself is not stored.

We use this information only to provide Premium. It is not used for advertising or profiling.

One-time purchases (Terms of Use section 6a: car photo unlocks and search packs) go through the same store and RevenueCat, which receives the same kind of purchase record for them. How many pack searches you have used is counted only on your phone.

Premium's other features work on your device. Trip history (with the SARS logbook's tags and notes, and trips you add by hand), your fuel settings, the vehicles, costs and odometer readings you give the logbook, the photos of odometers and slips you take or choose for it, and any Arrive by or logbook reminder are kept only on your phone (with the fuel price alert on, your fuel type is also stored with this device's notification registration, section 4a). For the logbook's "From", a drive that starts at one of your saved places (such as Home or Work) is named after it, on your phone. Tsamaya asks for your camera only when you tap to photograph an odometer or a slip, and a photo you choose instead is copied into the app; the photos are never uploaded. To measure a trip you add by hand, a place you search for is looked up like any search (section 1), the two places you pick are sent to Mapbox for a route, as for any route, and only their names are kept with the trip. A logbook you export, and its photos, are files that go only where you send them. Fuel cost and toll prices (for the vehicle class you choose) read the official fuel prices and toll tariffs from our public lists on our server, which contain no information about you, and Arrive by asks Mapbox how long the route takes at the time you would leave.

## 7. Security

We protect the information we hold with transport encryption (HTTPS/TLS) on all network calls and row-level security on our hosted database. Reports are stored under insert-only access rules: the app's public key cannot read them back. Only authorised reviewers can read reports, and only through accounts restricted to that work. Our providers are bound by their own data-protection terms. No system is perfectly secure, and we cannot guarantee absolute security.

If we have reasonable grounds to believe that someone has gained unauthorised access to personal information we hold, we will notify the Information Regulator and, where we can identify and reach the people affected, them, as POPIA section 22 requires.

## 8. Your rights (POPIA)

You have the right to:

- ask whether we hold personal information about you, and for a copy of it;
- ask us to correct or delete it;
- object to our processing it, including for direct marketing (such as app news notifications), and withdraw any consent you gave; many of these choices are also switches in the app (location, notifications, usage events, natural voice);
- not be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing that affects you significantly;
- complain to the Information Regulator: inforegulator.org.za, POPIAComplaints@inforegulator.org.za.

To exercise any right, email info@tsamayaapp.co.za, or contact us in any other way that suits you. It is free. Because Tsamaya has no accounts, we find what we hold through your installation: tell us what you sent and roughly when, or send us your **Installation ID**, which you can copy from Settings › Help & About. We may ask for this to confirm that a request comes from the installation concerned. We respond within 30 days. Because we hold little personal information about drivers, many requests will be answered by confirming what we hold for your installation, which is often nothing beyond what is on your phone.

## 9. Children

Tsamaya is a driving app for people aged 18 or older and is not directed at children. We do not knowingly process children's personal information. If you believe a child has sent us a report or shared a trip, contact us and we will delete it.

## 10. Risk data is not personal data

The risk zones and road classifications shown in the app are derived from public, aggregated sources (including SAPS crime statistics and OpenStreetMap) plus curated review. They describe **places**, never individuals, and contain no personal information.

## 11. Our website

Our website, tsamayaapp.co.za, is hosted on GitHub Pages and counts visits with Cloudflare Web Analytics, which sets no cookies and stores nothing on your device. It records the pages visited, the referring site, browser and device type and country, to show us how many people visit and which pages they read; it is not used to identify you. The hosting provider receives your IP address with each request, as any website does. The contact page opens your own email app; the website itself collects nothing you type.

The trip page (track.html) shows a shared trip (section 3a) to whoever holds its link: the viewer's browser fetches the trip from our server and draws it on a map from Mapbox, so Mapbox receives the viewer's network address and the map area around the trip. We do not record who views it.
