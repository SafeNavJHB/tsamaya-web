## 1. Introduction

1.1 Tsamaya is a navigation app for South African metros. It suggests driving routes with lower statistical exposure to the areas and roads that our statistical risk data rates as higher risk. This policy explains what Personal Information the Tsamaya App and our Website (tsamayaapp.co.za) Process, why, who receives it, how long we keep it, and your rights under the Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 (POPIA). Earlier versions are listed in clause 24.

It should be read together with our Terms of Use, which govern your use of the App, and our PAIA Manual, which describes the records we hold and how to request access to them. Both are available on the Website.

1.3 Tsamaya is the Responsible Party for the Personal Information described in this policy and decides why and how it is Processed. Where a provider listed in clause 13 Processes Personal Information on our behalf, it does so as our Operator.

## 3. Summary

3.1 Tsamaya uses your location on your phone to show the map, guide you and plan routes. For this, coordinates are sent to the services that answer each request: Mapbox for maps and routes; Google, HERE or Mapbox for place search; and our own server, to find police, roadblock and pothole notices near you and for the rain reminder. They are sent without your name or an account. We keep no history of where you are or where you go, apart from what you choose to send us (clauses 6 and 7).

3.2 Drivers do not have accounts. We show no advertising, we do not sell Personal Information and we do not track you across other apps.

3.3 Saved places (such as Home and Work), recent searches, saved routes and your trip history are stored only on your phone, and we cannot see them. A place name leaves your phone only in the ways this policy describes, including a search, the natural voice saying where you have arrived (clause 9), a live trip you choose to share (clause 7) and a report you send (clause 6).

3.4 Each installation of Tsamaya has an Installation ID. It is not your name or an account, but it links the usage events, crash reports, reports, notification registration and shared trips that come from one installation, so we treat it as Personal Information.

3.5 We collect as little as we can and, wherever we can, we de-identify information so that it can no longer be linked to an installation or a person; information de-identified in that way, such as the aggregate counts and statistics we keep, is not Personal Information. Information that can still be linked to an installation or a person, such as the Installation ID, network addresses, a shared trip, a report that carries a position and an email address you choose to give us, is Personal Information, and we handle it as this policy describes.

3.6 We collect usage events (such as a route being accepted) and crash reports. They carry that identifier, but never your name or where you went. Our crash reporting service also counts app sessions under its own random identifier, so that we know how often the App crashes. You can switch usage events off in Settings › Sharing & privacy (clause 10).

3.7 If you send a report or feedback, we store what you submit, and your email address only if you choose to give it (clause 6).

3.8 Voice reports are optional. When you tap Speak (or ask Siri) to report something on the road, your phone's own speech service turns what you say into text so that the App can choose the report. Tsamaya never records or keeps audio; only the report itself is sent (clause 8).

3.9 Tsamaya Premium is optional. If you subscribe, Apple or Google takes the payment and we never see your card. RevenueCat, the service that checks subscriptions for us, receives your store purchase records and a random id it creates, so that Premium stays on the account that paid (clause 12).

3.10 Notifications are optional. Nothing arrives unless your phone allows Tsamaya to send notifications, and news about new features arrives only if you ask for it. We store a delivery token for your device and the metro you are in (such as "Cape Town"), never your coordinates. With the fuel price alert on, we also store your fuel type and, with Tsamaya Premium, a date based on when your Premium ends. Each kind of notification has its own switch (clause 11).

## 6. User Reports and Feedback

6.1 You can suggest updates to risk areas and roads, rate trips, report what you see on the road, and send feedback or bug reports without an account. When you submit a report, we store:

6.1.1 your selections: the risk tier you suggest, the kinds of incident you select, and the timing you choose;

6.1.2 whatever you type in the note or message field (**please do not include Personal Information about yourself or others**); a report made during a drive fills in the road you were driving on, and one made from the car screen or through Siri also your speed and direction of travel at that moment;

6.1.3 the area or road the report concerns (and any line or area you draw, with the points you tapped), the time band, and the App version;

6.1.4 an Installation ID generated on your device, used to spot duplicate or abusive submissions and to find your reports if you ask us about them; it is not an account and identifies the installation, not you;

6.1.5 your email address, only if you choose to give it, used only to reply to that report.

6.2 Trip ratings store coarse trip statistics (such as a distance bucket, the time band, the route option and whether a reroute happened) and, for each rated area or road the drive passed through, its name and your answer, but never your start or end locations. A rating is sent when you tap Good or Not good (with any details you then add), or when you close the rating after answering at least one question.

6.3 "This route looks wrong" reports store your comment, the destination's name, the route option, its distance and time, the metro, the time band, the App version and the Installation ID, together with the start and end rounded to about 100 m, the lines of the routes we offered without their first and last 200 m, and the names of the risk areas and roads each route avoided or passed. The review team uses them to find and fix routing mistakes.

6.4 A navigation log you send from Settings › Help & About (Help & feedback) helps us find a fault. You choose the version without locations, or the full log, which includes positions and place names from your recent drives. It goes by email to admin@tsamayaapp.co.za with your Installation ID and a summary of your settings.

6.5 Reports are suggestions for human review; they never change the live dataset automatically, except for speed cameras: a camera reported at the same spot from three different installations is added to the map without waiting for review, and drivers' answers to "still there?" on a reported camera extend, shorten or end how long it shows. Each such answer stores the Installation ID, so one installation counts once. When a report arrives, the review team is told by email (sent through Resend) and by a notification on their phones (sent through Expo), which can carry the report's text and place name.

6.6 Some reports are also checked by Claude, an AI assistant made by Anthropic, to help the review team: area and road reports and trip ratings with details automatically, and other reports, such as crime spot reports, when the team asks. We use Claude through Anthropic's API under Anthropic's commercial terms, which do not allow Anthropic to train its models on what it receives. Claude receives what the report says, its area, road or pinned point and the street address nearest to it, its timing, and what the App already holds there, such as the current rating or nearby hotspots. It never receives your Installation ID or email address. Claude may search public sources, and its answer is advice for the review team: it never approves or declines a report by itself. Anthropic's own terms and Privacy Policy (www.anthropic.com/legal/privacy) apply to what it receives.

6.7 Please do not include Special Personal Information, such as information about anyone's health or alleged criminal behaviour, or the name of any person, in a report or note. A crime spot report concerns a place, not a person. If a report contains Special Personal Information or other Personal Information about an identifiable person, the review team will remove it.

6.8 Some kinds of report carry a position, because the position is what is reported:

6.8.1 Police, roadblock and pothole notices are public: other drivers nearby see "Police reported ahead" for about an hour, and "Pothole reported ahead" for about four days. We store the spot where your car was when you reported, its direction of travel and the time. Your Installation ID and a scrambled form of your network address (a hash under a secret key that only our server holds, so nobody without that key can turn it back into the address) are kept apart from the notice, only to merge repeat reports and to limit abuse, and are never shown to anyone. Notices are deleted within 48 hours after they expire. If you pass a police, roadblock or pothole notice another driver reported, Tsamaya may ask you "still there?". Your answer is stored with your Installation ID and the same kind of scrambled network address, only to count each installation once and to limit abuse; neither is ever shown to anyone, and both are deleted with the notice. Answering "Still there" keeps the notice up for another hour, up to four hours after it was first reported (a pothole notice for another four days, up to 30 days after it was first reported). A roadblock report is also filed for the review team as a report under this clause (the road you were on, from the car screen or Siri your speed and direction, the time band and your Installation ID), kept as other reports are (clause 15).

6.8.2 Speed camera reports store the spot you reported, the kind of camera, the metro, the speed limit if you gave one, any comment you add, and the Installation ID, for the review team, which confirms or rejects the camera (clause 15 says how long the report is kept).

6.8.3 Crime spot reports (a hijacking or smash-and-grab spot you pin on the map) and suggested corrections to a hotspot's position store the spot you pinned, not where you are, with the place name nearest to it, the kind of spot, when it happened if you say, your comment and the Installation ID, for the review team. An approved spot is shown on every driver's map with a name and safety tip chosen by the review team; your comment and identifier are never shown.

## 7. Live Trip Sharing, SOS and Guardian (optional)

7.1 You can choose to let someone follow your drive on a private web link. Nothing is shared unless you start it:

7.1.1 Share, during a drive, creates a link for that trip and opens your phone's share sheet, so that you choose who receives it.

7.1.2 SOS › Alert creates an emergency link and opens your phone's messaging app with a message to your emergency contact, which you send yourself. The message contains your exact position as a Google Maps link and the emergency link.

7.1.3 Guardian uses one lasting link for your installation, which you send once to someone you trust. After that, each time you switch Guardian on (for example from the car screen), your drive is published to that same link until you switch it off or arrive.

7.2 While a trip is shared, we store on our server (Supabase) your current location and heading, your destination (its position and name) and estimated arrival time, the route line you are driving (which starts where you set off, and so shows your starting point) and the Installation ID, but never your name or an account. If someone opens the link inside the Tsamaya App, their own Installation ID is stored with the trip so that their app can follow it. Only someone who holds the link (a random token that cannot be guessed) can see the trip, so share it only with people you trust.

7.3 When you end the trip or arrive, live updates stop and the private link expires 30 minutes later. While a trip is still active, the link can be viewed for up to 12 hours after your last update (SOS links for up to 24 hours, so that a helper can keep checking on you). We delete the trip's stored location, destination and route within 30 days after its link stops working, and sooner on request via the contact address. Apart from police, roadblock and pothole reports and the reports in clause 6 that carry a position, this is the only case where your location is stored on our server.

## 8. Voice Reports and Siri (optional)

8.1 If you tap Speak in the report screen (on your phone, CarPlay or Android Auto), Tsamaya asks for microphone and speech recognition access, then listens for a few seconds for what you say, such as "police" or "speed camera". Your phone's own speech service turns the words into text: Apple's speech recognition on an iPhone, and the speech recognition service on an Android phone (usually Google's). Depending on the phone and the language, that service may Process the audio on the phone or on the provider's servers, under the provider's own privacy terms.

8.2 Tsamaya uses the text only to choose the report type, and then files exactly the report a tap would (see clause 6). **We do not record, store or send the audio, and we do not keep the text.** If you ask Siri instead ("report police in Tsamaya"), Siri does the listening and passes Tsamaya only the report type. If you ask Siri for directions in Tsamaya, Siri (Apple) hears the place you name and passes it to Tsamaya, which looks it up like any search (clause 4). On Android Auto, a destination you say is heard by Google Assistant, which passes Tsamaya the words in the same way.

8.3 You can refuse or withdraw microphone and speech access at any time in your phone's settings. Every report can still be made by tapping.

## 9. Spoken Directions (natural voice)

9.1 With Natural voice on (Settings › Navigation, on unless you switch it off), each sentence Tsamaya speaks is made by Google Cloud Text-to-Speech through our server. Most sentences are directions such as "Turn left onto Jan Smuts Avenue". The closing sentences say where you have arrived, but in this voice the only destination or stop names they use are Home and Work: any other destination or stop (a street address, a place you named) is "your destination" or "your stop", so it never leaves your phone this way. Your phone's own voice, which sends nothing, still says the name.

9.2 Our server keeps the audio of each sentence in a cache it shares between drivers, so that the same sentence is never made twice. The cache stores a sentence's sound under a code made from its words, with no Installation ID and nothing else about you or your device, and it cannot be listed or browsed. Google receives only the sentence, from our server, never anything about you. Versions of the App before its October 2026 update could also send the name of a destination or stop in the closing sentence. Our server now answers such a sentence with the same sentence without the name ("You have arrived at your destination. Go well."), so the name is never made into speech or stored. Clips those versions made earlier remain in the cache, which cannot be listed and links them to no one. Switch Natural voice off and Tsamaya uses your phone's own voice, which sends nothing.

## 10. Usage Analytics and Crash Reporting

10.1 Tsamaya records usage events so that we can see whether people find the App useful, and what to improve or fix. Examples include the App being opened, a route being requested, shown, accepted, started, completed or abandoned, a reroute, a report being sent, a route being shared, and the Premium screen being shown or a purchase being made. These events carry only short, coarse details (such as the metro you are in, the route option chosen, rounded distances and durations, the safety grades of the options, and whether a route was rerouted), together with the Installation ID described in clause 6, a random number for that app session, the App version, the build channel and the platform. They never include your name, an account, your start or end locations, place names, or precise coordinates. We also keep a record of each installation (when it was first seen, its platform, app version and metro) so that we can count installations. We use these events to understand use in aggregate, to tell the team when a new installation first opens the App (with its platform and metro), and to recognise app-store review and test devices (clause 5); never to profile you for advertising or to decide anything else about you. **You can switch usage events off at any time** in Settings › Sharing & privacy (Share usage data).

10.2 Crash reporting is switched on. When the App crashes or meets an unexpected error, it sends a technical report to Sentry so that we can find and fix the fault. So that we can see how often the App crashes, the crash reporting software also tells Sentry when each app session starts and ends, and whether it ended in a crash, with the App version and a random identifier Sentry's software creates for this installation (not the Installation ID in clause 6); this happens whether or not usage events are switched on.
