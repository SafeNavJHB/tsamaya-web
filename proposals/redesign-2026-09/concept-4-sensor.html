Inside the app

What happens after you tap Go

  1. 01

    Pick where you're going

    Search, tap the map, or press and hold to drop a pin.

  2. 02

    Test the quickest route

    Tsamaya takes the quickest route and tests it against every rated area, using the ratings for this hour.

  3. 03

    Move it onto checked roads

    If the route runs through a high-risk area, it moves it onto roads we have checked, and tells you what it went around.

  4. 04

    Throw out bad detours

    A detour only gets offered if it cuts your exposure. One that adds too much distance is thrown out, even when it carries less risk.

  5. 05

    Say so when there's no better way

    When there is no good alternative, Tsamaya says so and gives you the normal route with the risky stretches marked. It will not invent a detour to look busy.

  6. 06

    Drive it

    Turn-by-turn with voice, CarPlay and Android Auto in the app. Or hand it to Google Maps with the detour points already in place.

Where the ratings come from
Published South African crime statistics, scored against OpenStreetMap road data to find where vehicle crime concentrates. Every metro gets a review pass before anything goes live, and drivers correct it from there.

Rated by the hour
Every area carries three ratings: daytime 05:00 to 17:30, evening 17:30 to 19:30 and night 19:30 to 05:00. Yellow means caution: it is shown on the map but never forces a detour.

Gauteng · 800 rated areas

Johannesburg

Johannesburg is where Tsamaya started and it is still the busiest part of the map. Almost everything funnels through a handful of roads: the M1 and M2, Empire, Louis Botha, Oxford. Those roads run close to the places where vehicle crime concentrates. Plan purely for speed and you will be sent through the worst of it at the worst time of night, without being told.

Driving here

What to know on these roads

  1. 01

    The M1 and M2 through the inner city carry more flagged stretches than any other road we map. Most of it is smash-and-grab and robbery at off-ramps and traffic lights.

  2. 02

    Ratings move sharply between day and night here. Several roads that carry no penalty at midday sit in the top band after 19:30.

  3. 03

    Load-shedding matters. A dark intersection is a different proposition from a lit one, which is why evening and night are rated separately.
