Street guide · 12 metros · 4 396 rated areas · Edition 2026/09/24

The street guide that knows what time it is.

Tsamaya plans your drive around the areas where vehicle crime happens in 12 South African metros. It reads the clock first, because the ratings change at 17:30 and again at 19:30.

Tsamaya (say: tsa-MAH-ya) is Sesotho and Setswana for go.

C3 How to use this guide

You do steps 1 and 6. Steps 2 to 5 happen on their own once you press Go.

  1. Set a destination. Search for it, tap the map, or press and hold to drop a pin.
  2. Tsamaya tests the quickest route. It checks the line against every rated area, using the ratings for this hour.
  3. It moves off high-risk ground. If the route runs through a high-risk area, Tsamaya moves it onto roads we have checked and tells you what it went around.
  4. A detour has to earn its place. It is only offered if it cuts your exposure. One that adds too much distance is thrown out, even when it carries less risk.
  5. No good alternative? It says so. You get the normal route with the risky stretches marked.
  6. Drive it. Turn-by-turn with voice in the app, on CarPlay or on Android Auto. Or hand it to Google Maps with the detour points already in place.

F2 Notes for drivers

The four questions we answer most, including the one about guarantees.

Is it free?

Yes. No ads, and we do not sell anything about you.

Does a lower-risk route take much longer?

Usually a few minutes. Anything dramatically longer than the direct route is rejected.

Does it guarantee I will be safe?

No, and we will never tell you otherwise. It cuts your exposure to areas with a history of vehicle crime. Crime is not predictable. Treat it as better information for a choice you were going to make anyway, and stay alert.

What happens outside the 12 metros?

It works as a normal map and navigator, without risk data.

H2 Corrections welcome

Old street guides printed a slip at the back asking readers to send in mistakes. This is ours. The free ways come first.

Correction slip

  1. Drive with it. Use it on the trips you already make, at the hours you make them.
  2. Tell one other driver. Tsamaya is independent and self-funded, so word of mouth is the whole marketing plan.
  3. Report a corner we got wrong. Tap the road or the area in the app and send a report from there.
  4. Tell us when it annoys you. A detour you would never take is worth hearing about.

Johannesburg is where Tsamaya started and it is still the busiest part of the map. Almost everything funnels through a handful of roads: the M1 and M2, Empire, Louis Botha, Oxford. Those roads run close to the places where vehicle crime concentrates. Plan purely for speed and you will be sent through the worst of it at the worst time of night, without being told.

Driving notes

  1. The M1 and M2 through the inner city carry more flagged stretches than any other road we map. Most of it is smash-and-grab and robbery at off-ramps and traffic lights.
  2. Ratings move sharply between day and night here. Several roads that carry no penalty at midday sit in the top band after 19:30.
  3. Load-shedding matters. A dark intersection is a different proposition from a lit one, which is why evening and night are rated separately.

Questions about Johannesburg

Does it cover Soweto and the southern suburbs?

Yes. Coverage spans the whole municipal area.

Will it route me the long way round?

Only within limits. Most lower-risk routes across Johannesburg add a few minutes.

Does it work on the highways out of Joburg?

Yes. The N1, N3, N12 and N17 are mapped, and the major freeways carry protective ratings, so the app will not push you off a freeway onto side roads to shave a score.
